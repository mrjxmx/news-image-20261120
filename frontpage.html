The Morning After · Shenzhen Summit

PUTIN RULES OUT A TRUCE AS EUROPE RALLIES TO KYIV

A day after the Shenzhen trilateral, the world split over what it meant. In Berlin, some 40 partners of the Coalition of the Willing — Merz hosting, Macron and Zelenskyy beside him, US envoys attending, Trump on the phone — demanded a European seat at any peace table, backed security guarantees for Ukraine and discussed a proposed €45bn EU loan.

The E3 — Britain, France, Germany — called for a winter ceasefire on energy strikes. From Moscow: no sea or energy truce, Putin said, after Ukrainian forces hit the Kerch Bridge again overnight. Zelenskyy called the strikes retaliation for attacks on Ukraine’s grid. Shenzhen’s declaration never mentioned Ukraine or Iran; Trump’s embrace of Xi and Putin drew pushback at home.

The Hague · World Court

MYANMAR COMMITTED GENOCIDE, COURT RULES

The world court found, 9–6, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017 — the first time the world court has held a state responsible for genocide. Judges cited 10,000 killed and 730,000 driven into Bangladesh, and ordered reparations.

Middle East · Hormuz

HORMUZ ATTACKS RISE, OIL FALLS

Iran has mounted about 14 attacks on shipping in and near Hormuz this month, shifting to cruise missiles after Muscat talks collapsed. Yet Brent has slid a fifth in November, to about $92, as the IEA sees supply outrunning demand.

United Nations

NO SUCCESSOR FOR GUTERRES

The race to lead the UN has stalled: after eight rounds of voting, the Security Council’s five veto powers cannot agree on who follows António Guterres — the UN could soon be leaderless.
